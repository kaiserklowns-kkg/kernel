# Oceans 0.1.0 alpha: release notes (draft)

**Not yet released.** This page is a draft. A release needs:

- a build with the release key (see "Before publishing");
- the checks below on real machines.

## What it is

The first Oceans you can boot on a PC from a USB stick. It is for
developers and testers. Expect crashes; do not keep anything you cannot
lose on it.

## What is in it

**Machine and drivers.** A Rust microkernel (ABI 29) that uses every CPU
(ADR-0088, ADR-0089; a process killed at any moment ends, ADR-0104), on Tier 1 hardware
([compatibility](../hardware/compatibility.md)):
- storage: NVMe and SATA (AHCI);
- input and USB: xHCI USB keyboards and mice, USB sticks; the PS/2
  keyboard and a laptop's PS/2 touchpad or a PS/2 mouse (ADR-0132);
- network: Intel 82574L, I217/I218/I219, 82576, I210/I211, I225/I226, and Realtek
  RTL8111/8168 (ADR-0041, ADR-0123, ADR-0140; the I219 runs on an HP ZBook,
  the 8168 not yet tried on a real machine);
- Wi-Fi: the Intel Wireless 8260, WPA2-Personal and open networks, chosen
  in Control Center (ADR-0129, ADR-0130; not yet tried on a real machine);
- sound: Intel High Definition Audio, output and input (ADR-0079,
  ADR-0087);
- display: the UEFI framebuffer; a laptop's brightness on Intel graphics from
  Haswell to Kaby Lake (ADR-0147);
- power: switching off (ACPI S5) and restarting (ADR-0085).

**Userspace.**
- init with service supervision and a shell with a history (Up, Down) and
  Tab completion (ADR-0133);
- crash-consistent root filesystem, sized for the whole disk (files up to
  256 MiB, ADR-0134; format 3, ADR-0142); FAT on removable media;
- IPv4/IPv6, TCP, DNS, TLS; the clock set from network time, and the
  time zone in Settings (ADR-0143).

**Apps.**
- Oceans Core runs signed apps, with declared permissions and consent
  prompts.
- Apps can be native (Rust), Go (wasm) or web (SvelteKit + Bun).
- The Store installs them, with confirmation in a system dialog.
- Apps come with the system: Calculator, Settings, Files, Activity
  Monitor, Text Editor (ADR-0080..0084), Image Viewer, for PNG and BMP
  pictures (ADR-0090), and Music, for MP3 and WAV files and radio
  stations online (ADR-0094, ADR-0135).
- Apps may play sound (`sound`, not asked); they cannot record.
- Settings shows the network and the sound devices (ADR-0096); changing
  them is still done in the shell.

**AI.** An agent runtime with approval-gated tools and an OpenAI-compatible
model gateway.

**Desktop.** A light desktop with a menu bar, a dock, a panel of apps,
windows that move and minimize, a Terminal, notifications, and Restart
and Shut Down (ADR-0085), with
interface text in Noto Sans and Thai (ADR-0076..0078). Text is copied
and pasted with Ctrl+C, X and V, and Ctrl+Shift+V in the Terminal; only
the app the user pastes into reads it (ADR-0095). Text Editor, Files, Image
Viewer and Activity Monitor can be resized by their edges and maximized
(ADR-0097). Files opens a file in the app for its kind, or one chosen
(ADR-0099), and renames, copies and moves files and folders (ADR-0145). The
sound has a volume and a mute in the menu bar, kept across
reboots (ADR-0100). The keyboard's volume keys change it (ADR-0101). The media
keys reach Music from any app (ADR-0102). The sound panel shows what
plays, with its buttons (ADR-0103). Apps can be used from the keyboard
alone: Tab and Shift+Tab move between their controls (ADR-0106).
Windows go side by side: dragged to the screen's sides and corners, or
with Super and the arrows; Super+F shows one full screen (ADR-0107).
Super alone opens the search: a few letters find an app or a file of
Home, and Enter opens it (ADR-0108). A right click keeps an app in the
dock, across reboots (ADR-0109). With a password the screen starts
locked, and Super+L locks it (ADR-0110); Settings' Account sets it, and
changes it only with the current one (ADR-0111). It also locks after a
while without input, ten minutes unless chosen, and from Lock in the
apps panel (ADR-0112). Settings chooses a light or dark appearance and
one of six accents, for the desktop and every app (ADR-0113). Thai is typed
beside English, switched with Super+Space or in the menu bar (ADR-0114). Several
people can share a computer: the administrator adds users in Settings,
each with a password and their own Home, chosen at the lock screen
(ADR-0115). Each keeps their own appearance, dock and keyboard
layout (ADR-0116). The taskbar is Wave, one round button at
the bottom that rises into the apps and windows under the pointer, and
Launchpad shows every app across the screen (ADR-0117). Control Center, from the status
icons at the top right, holds the quick settings, what plays and the
volume (ADR-0118). The bell at the top right shows the
notifications apps sent, newest first, with a count of new ones
(ADR-0119). Home and the apps kept in Wave sit on the
desktop too, opened with a double click (ADR-0120). The top bar is arranged as on
macOS, with a user's menu for locking and switching users (ADR-0121). The
interface is the same size on any screen: drawn sharp at a scale of 100 to
300 %, chosen from the panel's density or in Settings → Displays, and the
status icons read at a glance (ADR-0136). The web
experience (Control Center, Apps, AI Center, Settings) is served by the
bridge, for use from another device.

**Developers.** The SDK and the `oceans` tool: templates, keys, builds and
a local Store ([sdk](../../sdk/README.md)).

**Alpha tools.**
- `sysreport`: a hardware report (ADR-0068).
- `diag`: the kept log and crash reports, including the previous boot's
  (ADR-0070, ADR-0074).
- `update`: signed system updates into a second slot, with the previous
  release kept in the boot menu (ADR-0071).
- **Recovery** (ADR-0122): "(safe mode)" in the boot menu starts the
  usual system without the services kept to start at boot, and so does
  the boot after three that did not finish; "(recovery)" starts only the
  disks, the network and a shell, where `recovery` sets a forgotten
  password or resets the computer as new, and `update previous` puts the
  previous release first.

## Installing

**From Windows, without a USB stick** (ADR-0126 to ADR-0128): Oceans
replaces Windows and everything on its SSD.

1. Copy what you want to keep elsewhere.
2. Turn Secure Boot off in the firmware settings (Oceans is not signed by
   Microsoft yet).
3. Download `OceansSetup.exe` from the release and run it. Windows warns
   about an unknown publisher: the program is not signed yet.
4. Oceans Setup checks the PC, downloads Oceans and checks its signature.
   Type ERASE to go on: it prepares the installer and restarts.
5. The installer replaces Windows and restarts into Oceans.

Only a PC whose Windows is on an NVMe SSD, for now.

**From Linux, without a USB stick** (ADR-0131): Oceans replaces Linux and
everything on its SSD. With Secure Boot off, download `OceansSetup-linux`
from the release, then in a terminal:

```bash
chmod +x OceansSetup-linux
sudo ./OceansSetup-linux
```

It checks the PC, downloads Oceans and checks its signature, asks for
ERASE, puts the installer in Linux's EFI system partition (which needs
about 100 MB free), adds the boot entries and restarts. Only a PC whose
Linux is on an NVMe SSD, for now.

**From a USB stick:** download the release's files into one folder and
check them against the release key, which is in this repository
(`tools/keys/oceans-release.pub`) and not taken from the download
(ADR-0075):

```bash
cargo run -p oceans-dev -- verify DOWNLOADS --key 5fcfd97af4fd47b1406b085bd52f0f3da385977b6601c4473efc55c2992f87f0
```

Then write `oceans-0.1.0-alpha-usb.img` to a USB stick (Rufus, balenaEtcher
or `dd`).
Then boot a UEFI machine from it, with Secure Boot off (or, with
`…-secure-boot-usb.img`, on, after enrolling `oceans-secure-boot.cer`
from the stick in the firmware's `db`, ADR-0091). A blank NVMe SSD
becomes the root filesystem. Only a blank disk is formatted: a disk that
holds data is left alone.

## Known limits

- **Secure Boot** works with the image signed for it
  (`…-secure-boot-usb.img`), once its certificate
  (`oceans-secure-boot.cer`, on the stick) is enrolled in the firmware's
  `db`. It is updated in place like the plain image, with the stick
  given to `update` too:
  `run update out use:fs use:usbdisk -- apply /usb/oceans-….opk`
  (ADR-0092).
- Oceans does not do suspend,
  GPU acceleration or a hardware boot counter: three boots that did not
  finish bring safe mode (ADR-0122), but recovery mode and the previous
  release are chosen in the boot menu.
- **Switching off** (`shutdown`, or Shut Down in the apps panel) needs the
  firmware's `\_S5` as plain data; on a machine where it is AML code,
  Oceans says so and the power button is the way (ADR-0085).
- **Ethernet** (ADR-0123): the I219 and the RTL8168 are written from Intel's
  and BSD's drivers and tested only on the host, not yet on a machine.
  No RTL8125 (2.5G Realtek) yet.
- **Wi-Fi** (ADR-0129): the Intel Wireless 8260 only, tested on the host,
  not yet on a machine; WPA2-Personal (CCMP) and open networks only (no
  WPA3, enterprise, WEP or TKIP). With both a cable and Wi-Fi, the stack
  keeps one address: Wi-Fi replaces the wire once joined, and an address
  from another network stays until DHCP renews it. Started from a USB
  stick, the network is set again after each restart.
- **One release key:** it signs both the system and its first-party
  apps. It cannot be rotated or revoked yet.
- **Logs:** the current and the previous boot only (ADR-0074).
- **Music** plays MP3 and 16-bit PCM WAV (no AAC or FLAC yet), and MP3
  radio stations only (ADR-0094, ADR-0135). The volume is one for all
  sound: no per-app levels (ADR-0100).
- **The clipboard** holds text only (no pictures or files), and the
  Terminal cannot copy (ADR-0095).

## Before publishing

1. **The release key** (ADR-0072, ADR-0075, [tools/keys](../../tools/keys/README.md)):
   - made by the publisher with `oceans keygen`, kept outside the
     repository, with an offline backup. Its public half is published in
     `tools/keys/oceans-release.pub`.
   - Then, from the commit to tag:
     `OCEANS_RELEASE_KEY=PATH cargo xtask release`. It writes
     `build/release`: the image, the update `.opk`, `release.keys`,
     `BUILD-INFO`, `SHA256SUMS` and `SHA256SUMS.sig`. It refuses a key
     other than the published one.
   - Images from `cargo xtask usb` trust the public development key: they
     are for development only.
2. **Real machines:** at least one Tier 1 machine boots the release image
   (`build/release/oceans-0.1.0-alpha-usb.img`) and passes the validation
   in [compatibility.md](../hardware/compatibility.md#validating-a-machine).
3. **The release itself:**
   - a tag `v0.1.0-alpha`;
   - the files of `build/release` attached to a GitHub release;
   - CI green on the tagged commit.
